Why does losing $50 feel worse than finding $50 feels good? 🧠

Your brain isn't a fair calculator — it weighs losses about twice as heavily as equivalent gains. This "loss aversion," identified by Kahneman and Tversky, is why you hold onto bad stocks hoping to break even, stay in situations that aren't working, and feel a sting from a single criticism that no amount of praise can fully erase. The practical fix: when facing a decision, deliberately reframe it as what you're keeping rather than what you might lose.
